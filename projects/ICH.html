Project and Role Overview

Intracranial hemorrhages (ICH) are critical medical emergencies where rapid and precise diagnosis directly impacts patient survival. At the APAC Research Group, I served as Technical Manager and Deep Learning Engineer, leading the end-to-end development of AI-driven triage pipelines, volumetric hemorrhage quantification, and multi-specialist annotation tools across non-contrast head CT scan volumes.

The Clinical Challenge

Emergency radiologists must review dozens of 3D CT slices per patient under intense time constraints. The key bottlenecks include:

  • Severe Class Imbalance: Hemorrhage lesions occupy a tiny percentage of overall volumetric brain parenchyma.
  • High Annotation Burden: Pixel-level contour segmentation requires painstaking manual effort from certified radiologists.
  • Inter-Slice Volumetric Context: Isolated 2D slice classification loses vital spatial continuity across the axial scan stack.

Technical Methodology & Architecture

1. Transformer-Based Triage & CT-as-Video Modeling

To emulate how expert radiologists review consecutive slices, we formulated non-contrast head CT series as continuous volumetric video sequences. Using spatio-temporal Transformers, our architecture achieved a 98% F1 score on binary hemorrhage detection, capturing subtle inter-slice dependencies.

2. XAI & Generative AI for Annotation Independence

To overcome extreme medical data scarcity, we deployed Generative AI representations and Self-Supervised Pre-training to capture fundamental anatomical priors. Coupled with Grad-CAM visual heatmaps, the model generates trustworthy visual rationale for clinical decisions, slashing the dependency on expensive pixel-level manual segmentation masks.

3. Hemorrica Dataset Curation & Open-Source Annotation Software

Curated the Hemorrica dataset comprising curated 3D CT scans from 300+ patients with expert multi-sub-type hemorrhage labels.
