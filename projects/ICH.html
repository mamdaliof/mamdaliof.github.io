Intracranial Hemorrhage Diagnosis & Emergency Triage AI

Deep Learning Research Engineer at APAC Research Group (Jul 2022 to Sep 2023)

Project and Role Overview

Intracranial hemorrhages (ICH) are medical emergencies where fast and accurate diagnosis is critical for patient care. At the APAC Research Group, I built deep learning models and fuzzy decision rules to classify, localize, and segment brain hemorrhages across non-contrast head CT scan series.

Jul. 2022 to Sep. 2023 Peer-Reviewed Publication ResNet50 & Fuzzy Decision Rules Interactive Slider

The Challenge

Emergency radiologists often review dozens of 3D CT slices per patient under strict time limits. Severe class imbalance (far fewer slices show bleeding than healthy tissue) and subtle lesion boundaries make automated triage difficult.

Technical Approach and Methods

To tackle severe data scarcity and class imbalance, I deployed self-supervised pre-training and modern generative AI techniques to learn robust anatomical representations, drastically reducing dependency on extensive manual annotation.

For clinical explainability and patient-level triage, I paired Grad-CAM visual heatmaps with fuzzy decision rules that aggregate slice-level predictions across the full 3D scan volume, giving radiologists interpretable visual rationale and near-zero false-negative triage safety.
