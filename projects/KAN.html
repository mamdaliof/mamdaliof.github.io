Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) Review

Review, Implementations and Benchmarks of Edge-Learnable Spline Architectures

Research and Review Overview

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) represent an alternative to traditional Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). Based on the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem, KANs replace fixed activation functions on nodes with learnable 1D univariate spline functions parameterized on edges.

Pre-Print Review B-Splines & Radial Basis U-KAN Medical Segmentation Symbolic Interpretability

The Challenge

Standard deep neural networks rely on linear weight combinations followed by fixed activations (like ReLU or GELU). While effective, they act as uninterpretable black boxes and require large parameter counts to approximate non-linear mathematical physical laws accurately.

The Engineering & Review Approach

In this comprehensive review, I implemented, benchmarked, and evaluated KAN variants across multiple datasets:

  • Spline Formulations: Evaluated B-splines, Radial Basis Function (RBF) KANs, and Fractional KANs for continuous function fitting and parameter efficiency.
  • U-KAN for Medical Segmentation: Implemented KAN layers inside U-Net architectures, substituting standard bottleneck linear projections with learnable spline channels for biomedical imaging.
  • Symbolic Pruning and Interpretability: Demonstrated how KAN edge activations can be pruned to extract exact closed-form algebraic equations from trained networks.
